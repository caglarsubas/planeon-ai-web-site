## Design Context

### Users

Planeon serves three enterprise readers: executives who need to understand why multi-agent pilots stall, architects who need the complete system blueprint and boundaries, and engineers who need implementation-grade reference detail. The website must let each audience enter at the right depth while making the overall architecture credible and easy to cite.

### Brand Personality

Assured, precise, and composed. The voice is calm and technically authoritative without becoming cold, breathless, or promotional. Planeon should feel like an experienced architecture partner making a complex system legible.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light, editorial technical-document aesthetic aligned with the Planeon brand palette and logo. The user-requested high-end-visual-design revision adds soft structural surfaces: a floating pill navigation, concentric tray/core panels, asymmetrical reading paths, and generous whitespace. Keep Poppins headings; use self-hosted Geist for body and controls. Use diffused blue-ink ambient shadows, not harsh outlines. Preserve crisp geometric diagrams and plane colors for their semantic roles. Avoid neon effects, decorative charts, competing animations, and marketing claims about implementation maturity.

### Design Principles

1. Make the architecture understandable before making it detailed.
2. Preserve semantic meaning: runtime, knowledge, execution, and trust colors are not decorative accents.
3. Use restrained motion to explain sequence or state, with an equivalent static reduced-motion experience.
4. Maintain technical precision through readable type, stable numbering, aligned columns, and restrained nested framing. Keep working surfaces compact enough to use; macro spacing belongs to editorial sections.
5. Keep vendor-neutral framework content visually distinct from Planeon positioning.
